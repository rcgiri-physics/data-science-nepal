# 5. Building a portfolio and finding your community

## Why a portfolio

Degrees and certificates say what you studied; a **portfolio** shows what you can do. Employers, universities and
scholarship committees increasingly look at public projects. Your Grade 9–12 projects are already the start of one.

## What a good portfolio project looks like

* a **real question** that matters to someone (ideally about Nepal or your community);
* **real data**, credited and licensed properly;
* a clear **README**: question, data, method, results, limits, how to run it;
* clean, **reproducible** code (Grade 11 Unit 8), with a fixed seed and requirements file;
* **honest results**, including what didn't work;
* one or two **good charts** with action titles;
* a short **write-up** (blog post or PDF) a non-expert can read.

Three to five strong projects beat twenty half-finished ones.

## Where to publish

* **GitHub** (or GitLab, Codeberg): code, notebooks, README. A tidy GitHub profile with a short bio and pinned projects is
  your shop window.
* **Kaggle**: public notebooks on public datasets.
* **A simple website or blog**: GitHub Pages is free; write-ups with charts.
* **This curriculum's student gallery** (`community/student-gallery/`) for school projects, with consent.

Never publish personal data, restricted datasets (e.g. DHS microdata) or anything a client has not approved.

## Communities

Learning with others keeps you motivated and connects you to opportunities. Examples of organisations and communities
active in Nepal's data and technology space (activities change — check their current programmes before joining):

* **Open Knowledge Nepal** — open data, data literacy, the Open Data Nepal portal.
* **Kathmandu Living Labs** — open mapping, civic technology, OpenStreetMap work in Nepal.
* **CoSoG Nepal** — computer science and programming for school students.
* **Code for Nepal** — data and digital literacy projects.
* **NAAMII** (Nepal Applied Mathematics and Informatics Institute for Research) — AI research and schools/workshops.
* **Python, data and AI meetups** in Kathmandu and other cities; university clubs (computer, statistics, robotics).
* **Women in tech groups** and mentoring programmes.
* **Global online communities**: Kaggle discussions, Stack Overflow, the scikit-learn and pandas communities, PyData,
  OpenStreetMap.

How to participate well: ask clear questions (what you tried, the error, a small example), answer beginner questions when
you can, contribute documentation fixes to open-source projects, volunteer on mapathons, and present your projects at
meetups.

## Contributing to open source

You can start small: fix a typo in documentation, translate a page into Nepali, add an example. This curriculum itself
welcomes contributions (`CONTRIBUTING.md`). Open-source contributions are public evidence of skill and collaboration.
