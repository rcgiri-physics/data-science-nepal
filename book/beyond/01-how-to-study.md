# After Grade 12: how to keep learning

You have finished five years of data science at school. You can ask good questions, wrangle messy tables, write SQL and
Python, reason about uncertainty, build and audit simple models, and communicate honestly. This is a strong
**foundation** — and it is only the beginning. This part of the book is a guide to what comes next: how to study,
what to learn, which books and courses to use, how to show your work, and where it can lead.

> Verify every programme, entry rule, fee and scholarship on the official website before deciding. Offerings change
> every year; the names here are examples, not endorsements or guarantees.

# 1. How to study (what research says works)

Most students study by re-reading notes and highlighting. Research on learning shows these feel productive but work
poorly. These methods work much better:

## 1. Retrieval practice — test yourself

Close the book and try to recall: write down everything you remember about the bootstrap; answer the Check questions
without looking; explain a concept to a friend. Recalling strengthens memory far more than re-reading. Flashcards (paper
or an app such as Anki) are a simple tool.

## 2. Spaced repetition — come back later

Review a topic after a day, then after a few days, then after a week or two. Spreading practice over time beats
cramming the night before. Plan your week so old topics return.

## 3. Interleaving — mix problem types

Instead of 20 problems of one kind, mix them: a percentage-points question, then a regression interpretation, then a
SQL join. You learn to recognise *which* method a problem needs — the real skill.

## 4. Elaboration and explanation

Ask "why does this work?" and "how does this connect to what I know?" Write explanations in your own words. Teaching
someone else (a younger sibling, a study group) is one of the best ways to learn.

## 5. Learning by doing — projects

In data science, you learn most by working on real data with a real question. Every new technique should be tried on a
dataset you care about (Appendix A lists twenty free ones).

## 6. Deliberate practice with feedback

Work at the edge of your ability, on specific weaknesses, with feedback: online judges for SQL and Python, code reviews
from friends, comparing your solution with others'.

## Practical habits

* **A learning log.** One page per week: what I learned, what confused me, what I built.
* **Read documentation.** pandas, scikit-learn and Python documentation are excellent; learn to search them before
  asking.
* **Debug patiently** (Grade 9 Unit 4 routine): read the error, form a hypothesis, test one change.
* **Use AI assistants as tutors** — explanations, hints, practice questions, code review — not as replacements for
  thinking. Verify what they say.
* **Sleep, exercise and breaks** matter for memory. Study in focused blocks (e.g. 45 minutes + 10 minutes break).
* **Learn in public.** Write short posts about what you learn; share notebooks. Explaining clearly is a skill employers
  value.
* **Be patient with English.** Most resources are in English. Read slowly, keep a vocabulary list, and use translation
  tools for difficult passages; your skill will grow quickly with practice.
