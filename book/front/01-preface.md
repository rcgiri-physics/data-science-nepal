# Preface

Nepal produces more data every year than ever before: a census every ten years, daily rainfall from hundreds of
stations, prices at Kalimati market, air-quality sensors in the Kathmandu valley, school records, health-post
registers, mobile money, maps drawn by volunteers after the 2015 earthquake. Decisions about where to build a school,
which ward needs a health worker, when to warn farmers about floods and how to spend a municipality's budget are
increasingly made — or should be made — with data.

Yet most students leave school knowing how to calculate a mean and draw a pie chart, but not how to ask a good
question, collect data fairly, clean a messy table, tell a real pattern from chance, or notice who is missing from a
dataset. This book is an attempt to close that gap.

## What this book is

*Data Science from Nepal* is a five-year course for Grades 8 to 12. It was built for Nepal's schools, is open to
learners everywhere, and is written so that

* a **student** can follow it alone, chapter by chapter, with worked examples, exercises and answers to selected
  problems;
* a **teacher** can teach from it, with timed lessons, activities that work with or without computers, common
  misconceptions, full answer keys (in the teacher edition) and a handbook on teaching data-science foundations;
* a **curriculum planner** can see how ideas build from Grade 8 to Grade 12 and how they connect to existing Maths,
  Science, Social Studies and Computer Science subjects.

It is built around **Nepali contexts and data**: districts and provinces, the Terai–Hill–Mountain belts, monsoon
rainfall, Kathmandu's winter air, migration and remittances, maternal health, Bikram Sambat dates. Where real data can
be redistributed freely, exercises use it; where it cannot, the book ships small **synthetic** practice tables that
look like the real thing and are clearly labelled, and points you to the real source.

## How it is organised

* **Getting started** — how to use the book, how to set up (from paper to Python), and the data-science cycle that
  runs through every chapter.
* **Grade 8: Data detectives** — questions, collecting, graphs, summaries, comparisons, data stories and ethics,
  without any programming.
* **Grade 9: Data wranglers** — tidy spreadsheets, cleaning, SQL, first Python, pandas, charts and rates.
* **Grade 10: Data analysts** — cleaning real series, distributions, relationships, time series, a first model, AI
  literacy and dashboards.
* **Grade 11: Data scientists I** — performance, advanced SQL, uncertainty and the bootstrap, testing, regression,
  seasonality, maps and reproducibility.
* **Grade 12: Data scientists II** — the machine-learning workflow, evaluation, overfitting, fairness audits, feature
  engineering, communication and a capstone with a real client.
* **Teaching data-science foundations** — a handbook for teachers: the progression of ideas, planning a year,
  teaching statistics and code, low-resource classrooms, projects, assessment, AI and inclusion.
* **After Grade 12** — how to keep learning: study methods, a roadmap, books, courses, building a portfolio,
  communities, and pathways into higher education and work.
* **Appendices** — free datasets, an English–Nepali glossary, cheat sheets for spreadsheets, SQL and Python, and key
  formulas.

## What it asks of you

Curiosity, honesty and patience. Data science is not mainly about clever code. It is about asking a clear question,
looking carefully, being honest about what the data can and cannot show, and remembering that most data are about
people.

## How this book was made

This book began as a curriculum for Nepal's schools and is open to learners everywhere. It was structured and drafted
with the assistance of AI, then curated, verified and adapted by the author: every worked example is run
automatically, and sources are credited in the project's third-party log. If you find a passage that should credit
someone, please report it through the repository so it can be fixed.
