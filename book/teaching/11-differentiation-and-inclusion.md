# 11. Differentiation and inclusion

## Different starting points

Students arrive with very different Maths confidence, English reading levels and computer experience. Strategies used
throughout the teacher guides:

**Support**
* partially filled tables and sentence starters ("The median is ___ because ___");
* worked examples before independent tasks; one step at a time;
* paper version before the computer version;
* pairing with a supportive partner in the data-steward role;
* glossary in Nepali and English (Appendix B); allow explanations in any language.

**Stretch**
* ★★★ exercises and real-data exercises;
* "What would change your conclusion?" and "Who is missing?" questions;
* extension tasks: a second dataset, a different method, a dashboard;
* mentoring younger students.

## Language

The curriculum is written in English with Nepali key terms; Nepali chapter translations are being prepared
(`community/translations/STATUS.md`). In class:
* explain concepts in the language students think in; keep technical terms (column, median, query) consistent;
* code keywords are English (`SELECT`, `for`) — teach them as vocabulary;
* data can be in any language: Devanagari text in CSVs works in all tools if saved as UTF-8.

## Gender

Girls are under-represented in computing in Nepal as elsewhere. What helps:
* deliberate rotation of keyboard roles;
* examples that matter to everyone (health, environment, community);
* visible role models (invite women working in data, statistics, public health, engineering);
* watch participation in discussions and lab access;
* make the "Who is missing?" habit apply to the classroom too.

## Students with disabilities

* Visual: large fonts; high-contrast and colour-blind-safe charts (viridis palettes); describe charts in words; screen
  readers work with notebooks and spreadsheets.
* Hearing: written instructions on the board; captioned videos.
* Motor: pair programming roles; keyboard shortcuts.
* Learning differences: chunk tasks; checklists; extra time; oral alternatives to written work.

## Students affected by the topics

Some datasets touch students' lives: the 2015 earthquake, migration of family members, poverty, health. Always offer an
alternative dataset, avoid singling anyone out, and use aggregated or synthetic data for practice.

## Rural and urban

Choose local contexts for hooks. A rural student's knowledge of crops, weather and markets is expertise — use it (Grade
12 capstones with cooperatives, community forest groups, health posts).
