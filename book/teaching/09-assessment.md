# 9. Assessment

The curriculum's assessment fits Nepal's continuous, competency-based approach: frequent low-stakes checks, projects
marked with a rubric, and oral checks.

## The assessment toolkit

| Tool | When | Purpose | Marked? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Check questions** (3 per lesson) | every lesson | retrieval practice; spot misconceptions | no (self-check) |
| **Starter quiz** | start of lesson | recall the previous lesson | no |
| **Unit quiz** (`quiz.yml`) | end of unit | check core ideas | low stakes |
| **Practice exercises** (★/★★/★★★) | homework | practice and stretch | selected, or peer-marked |
| **Real-data exercises** | homework / project | transfer to real data | as part of project |
| **Unit project** | end of unit | apply the whole cycle | rubric |
| **Oral check** (2 min) | every project | verify understanding; own the work | yes |
| **Observation checklist** | during activities | "I can" statements | records |
| **Portfolio** | across the year | growth and reflection | end of year |

## The six-criterion project rubric

From `docs/pedagogy/assessment.md`, used for every project:

| Criterion | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Secure | 4 Extending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | vague question | stated but not answerable with data | clear, answerable, local | also anticipates limits |
| Plan & Data | source unclear | source named; bias not discussed | source, sampling and consent described | also compares alternatives |
| Analysis | wrong or missing method | right method, errors | correct, readable graph/table | also checks outliers/rates/uncertainty |
| Conclusion | no evidence | claim without evidence | claim–evidence–reasoning | also states uncertainty and who is missing |
| Communication | hard to follow | partly clear | clear poster/notebook | engaging, accurate, accessible |
| Ethics & honesty | ignored | mentioned | privacy and fairness handled | also credits all sources and AI help |

Score each criterion 1–4 (total out of 24). Share examples of "Secure" work so students know what it looks like.

## The oral check

Two minutes per student (or group, with questions to individuals). Example questions:
* "Show me where you removed the −999 values. Why did you remove them?"
* "Why did you report the median and not the mean?"
* "What would change your conclusion?"
* "Who is missing from your data?"
* "Explain this line of code."

The oral check is essential now that AI tools can produce plausible work: it rewards understanding, not polish. Keep it
friendly; a student who cannot explain something gets a chance to revisit it.

## Marking code

Mark the **thinking**, not the elegance: does the code do what was planned? Is it readable (names, comments)? Does it
run from top to bottom (Restart & Run All)? Are results checked (`assert`, totals adding up)?

## Grading for growth

* Allow resubmission of one project per term after feedback.
* Keep a portfolio: the best project per unit with a short reflection. By Grade 12 it is evidence for university
  applications and jobs.

## Item writing for data science

Good quiz questions test **interpretation**, not only calculation:
* "A news site says cases doubled from 2 to 4. What question would you ask?"
* "Which summary would you report for monthly household income in a village, and why?"
* "A model has 85% accuracy. What else do you need to know?"
