# 12. Your own learning path and professional community

## A learning path for teachers

You do not need a degree in data science to teach this curriculum. A realistic path:

| Stage | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Before teaching Grade 8 | 6–8 hours | read Grade 8 as a student; do the Check questions and ★ exercises; try CODAP |
| 2. Before teaching Grade 9 | 15–20 hours | work through Grade 9 with a computer; free courses: Kaggle Learn "Python", "Pandas", "Intro to SQL" |
| 3. Before teaching Grade 10 | 20–30 hours | Grade 10 chapters; Khan Academy statistics (distributions, scatter plots); CS50P (selected weeks) |
| 4. Before teaching Grades 11–12 | 40+ hours | Grades 11–12 chapters; OpenIntro Statistics (foundations for inference, regression); Kaggle Learn "Intro to ML"; *An Introduction to Statistical Learning* chapters 2–5 |
| 5. Ongoing | an hour a week | one real-data exercise per month; a professional community (below) |

## The one-day training workshop

The outline in `docs/for-teachers/README.md` (PPDAC as learners, teach-back, spreadsheets and cleaning, unplugged and
coded SQL, assessment and AI policy, planning) can be run by a district resource person or a lead teacher. Teachers
experience the lessons as students first — the most effective form of professional development.

## Professional learning communities

* Meet once a month (in person or online) with other teachers using the curriculum: share a lesson that worked, one that
  didn't, and student work.
* Co-plan a unit; observe each other's lessons with a simple focus ("How many students made a prediction?").
* Keep a short teaching log per unit (time taken, what confused students, what you changed).

## Contributing back

The curriculum improves through teachers' feedback:
* open a *teacher-feedback* issue on the project's repository, or send notes to the maintainer;
* contribute translations, local examples, datasets (with licence checks) and new exercises (`CONTRIBUTING.md`);
* pilot schools follow the protocol in `pilots/README.md` and publish results so that CDC and other schools can learn.

## Running a pilot in your school

1. Choose one or two units (recommended first: Grade 8 Unit 1 and Grade 9 Unit 3).
2. Record a baseline (a short pre-quiz from `quiz.yml`).
3. Teach the units; keep the teaching log.
4. Post-quiz; collect three examples of student work (anonymised); a short student survey.
5. Write the pilot report with `pilots/TEMPLATE-report.md`.
