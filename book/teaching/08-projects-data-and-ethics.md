# 8. Projects, student data and ethics

## Why projects

Every unit ends with a project, and every grade has a **flagship** project:

| Grade | Flagship |
|---|---|
| 8 | Our class, our school — a survey + district table → poster with claim and evidence |
| 9 | Which districts are changing? — district tables → poster + SQL report |
| 10 | Air in my city — a year of PM2.5 → report or dashboard |
| 11 | Climate or weather? — trend with uncertainty, reproducible in Git |
| 12 | Capstone with a client — scoping, model or analysis, audit, handover, public write-up |

Projects are where students practise the **whole** cycle, make decisions with no single right answer, and produce
something they can show. They also make the oral check meaningful.

## Running a project well

1. **Same five headings** (PPDAC) every time, from Grade 8 to 12; students learn the structure once.
2. **Plan card first** (question, data, variables, rate, risks); approve it before data work starts.
3. **Groups of three with rotating roles**; individual accountability through the oral check and a personal
   reflection.
4. **Milestones** (plan → clean data + log → analysis → draft poster → final) with quick feedback at each.
5. **Rubric shared at the start** (`docs/pedagogy/assessment.md`; capstone rubric in the capstone kit).
6. **Exhibit**: gallery walk, school assembly, a parents' day, or (with consent) the student gallery in the repository.

## Good project questions

* local, specific, answerable with available data, and genuinely interesting to students;
* about a group and variation (Grade 8 Unit 2);
* one sentence long.

Avoid questions that need private data, that rank individuals, or that could embarrass groups of students.

## Student data: consent and privacy

Class surveys are the most engaging data — and the most sensitive. Use the consent form in
`community/student-data-consent.md` and these rules:
* collect only what the question needs;
* codes, never names; the teacher keeps any key and destroys it at the end;
* aggregated results only in posters; small-group rule (no group smaller than 5);
* no photos of identifiable students without consent;
* sensitive topics (health, family income, caste, religion, disability) only with a strong reason, parents' consent and
  head teacher approval — usually better to use synthetic data;
* delete raw data at the end of the year.

## Real clients (Grade 12)

The capstone kit (`curriculum/grade-12/capstone-kit/`) has a client brief, data-agreement template, model-card template
and rubric. Teacher's responsibilities:
* approve every client and project;
* make sure no personal data reaches students without a signed agreement, head teacher approval and guardian consent
  where students appear; prefer aggregated or synthetic data;
* manage expectations: students deliver what they can in the time; a simple, honest result is a success;
* review public write-ups before publication.

## Ethics is not a separate lesson

Ethics appears in every grade (Grade 8 Unit 8, Grade 10 Unit 6, Grade 12 Unit 5) — but its real home is in every
lesson's reflection question and every project's limits box. Model it: when you present data, say who is missing.
