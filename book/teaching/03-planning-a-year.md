# 3. Planning a year

## How much time does a grade need?

Each grade has 8 units: 23–27 lessons plus 8 projects, about **40 periods** of 40–45 minutes. Real school years are
interrupted by exams, festivals (Dashain–Tihar leave alone takes weeks), strikes and weather, so plan for **32 teaching
weeks** at most.

## Five ways to schedule it

| Model | Periods | Suits | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Full course** | 1–2 periods/week for a year | schools offering it as a subject or optional | all units in order; projects in full |
| **B. Inside Maths** | ~15–20 periods replacing/extending statistics chapters | compulsory Maths, Grades 8–10 | Track A lessons only; short projects |
| **C. Term block** | 3 periods/week for one term | CS teachers, Grades 9–12 | core units + flagship project |
| **D. Club** | 1 period/week + Saturday sessions | ICT/science clubs | projects first, lessons as needed; real data exercises |
| **E. Intensive camp** | 5 days × 5 hours | holidays, teacher training | one flagship project per day, built around 2–3 units |

## Core and optional units

When time is short, teach the **core** units in each grade and treat the rest as extension.

| Grade | Core units (minimum) | Extension |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 | 6, 8 (but keep the ethics conversations in every unit) |
| 9 | 1, 2, 3 (or 5), 7, 8 | 4, 6 |
| 10 | 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 | 4, 7 |
| 11 | 3, 4, 5, 8 | 1, 2, 6, 7 |
| 12 | 1, 3, 5, 7, 8 | 2, 4, 6 |

## Sample plan: Grade 8 inside compulsory Maths (18 periods)

| Week | Periods | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | U1 L1, L3 (variables, reading tables) |
| 2 | 2 | U2 L1, L3 (statistical questions, fair samples) |
| 3 | 2 | U3 L1–L2 (consent, tally and frequency); collect class data |
| 4 | 2 | U3 L3, U4 L1 (tidy table; bar and dot plots) |
| 5 | 2 | U4 L2–L3 (line and pie; misleading graphs) |
| 6 | 2 | U5 L1–L2 (mean, median, mode; which summary) |

| 8 | 2 | U7 L1–L2 (CER, poster design) |
| 9 | 2 | Poster project + presentations (U7 project) |

## Sample plan: Grade 12 (one period per week, whole year)

Units 1–7 at one unit per month (with the unit project as homework), and the capstone running from month 6 to the
end, with milestone lessons (scoping, build and audit, handover).

## Planning a single unit

1. Read the chapter and teacher guide; note materials and the "no computers" version.
2. Decide which datasets: synthetic (always works) and one real-data exercise (if internet or a pre-downloaded file).
3. Prepare the hook with a **local** twist (your district, your school).
4. Book the computer lab, or prepare printed extracts and cards.
5. Decide the project format and deadline; share the rubric at the start.
6. After the unit, write three lines in a "teaching log": what worked, what confused students, what to change. Share it
   (see chapter 12 and `pilots/`).

## Multi-grade and shared teaching

* In schools where one teacher handles several grades, run **the same project theme** at different depths: a Grade 8
  group tallies travel modes; a Grade 10 group computes and charts rates; a Grade 12 group builds a dashboard.
* Older students can act as **data stewards** and mentors for younger ones — a strong way to consolidate their own
  learning.
