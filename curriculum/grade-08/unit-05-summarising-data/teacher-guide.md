## If you have no computers / no internet
All eight units run on paper, calculators and the blackboard. Computers are optional (spreadsheet or CODAP, https://codap.concord.org); every activity below has a paper version.

## Common misconceptions
* The mean is always the best 'average'. One extreme value can drag it.
* The mode is the 'biggest' value. It is the most *frequent*.

## Differentiation
* Support: give a partially filled table / sentence starters; pair with a data-steward role.
* Stretch: ask "what would change your conclusion?" and "who is missing?"

## Answer key (check questions)
**Lesson 1: Mean, median and mode**
1. Mean of 20, 20, 30, 40, 90?  
   *Answer:* 200 ÷ 5 = 40.
2. Median of 20, 20, 30, 40, 90?  
   *Answer:* 30.
3. Mode and range?  
   *Answer:* Mode 20; range 90 − 20 = 70.

**Lesson 2: Which summary when?**
1. Incomes: 9 × 15,000 and 1 × 300,000 — mean?  
   *Answer:* (135,000 + 300,000) ÷ 10 = 43,500.
2. Median of the same list?  
   *Answer:* 15,000.
3. Which is more typical and why?  
   *Answer:* The median: the mean is pulled up by one outlier.

**Lesson 3: Spread and what summaries hide**
1. Mean of class A (7, 8, 8, 9)?  
   *Answer:* 8.
2. Mean and range of class B (5, 6, 10, 11)?  
   *Answer:* Mean 8; range 6.
3. What do these tell us that the means alone do not?  
   *Answer:* B is much more spread out.
