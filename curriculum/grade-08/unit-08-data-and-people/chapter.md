# Grade 8 · Unit 8: Data and people

> Track: A (no-code) · Tools: paper, discussion · Lessons: 3 · Version: 0.1 draft (English)
> Datasets: `class-survey-synthetic`

**Big idea.** Behind every row is a person. Fair, careful data work asks who is counted, who is missing and who could be harmed.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can explain why privacy and consent matter.
* I can ask 'who is missing?' about a dataset.
* I can describe how data can be unfair.

**Words to know:** privacy, bias, fairness, missing, sensitive (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

---

## Lesson 1: Who is missing?

**Hook (5 min).** A phone survey about school uses asks only households with a phone. Who is never asked?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Datasets leave people out: those without phones, internet, documents, or those who were absent. Missing people can make conclusions unfair.

**Activity (20 min).** Read three case cards (a phone survey, an online poll, a school attendance report). For each list who is counted, who is missing, and how that may change the result.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Who might an online poll miss?
2. Why does missing data matter?
3. How could you include more people?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Privacy and sensitive data

**Hook (5 min).** Which facts about a person do you think should be private?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Sensitive data (health, caste/ethnicity, religion, disability, family income, exact location) can harm people if shared carelessly. Collect only what you need; anonymise; keep it safe.

**Activity (20 min).** Rank 10 data fields from 'fine to share' to 'private'; discuss why different people choose differently; create a 'collect only what you need' checklist.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Give two sensitive data fields.
2. What does 'collect only what you need' mean?
3. Small group, 'anonymous' data — risk?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Fair or unfair?

**Hook (5 min).** A school picks scholarship students using only exam marks. Is that fair to a child who works after school?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Data can hide unfairness. Ask: who collected it, what was measured, what was left out, who benefits, who is harmed. Fairness is a decision, not a number.

**Activity (20 min).** Debate three decisions that use data; use the five questions card to argue both sides; write a class charter 'Our data rules'.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Name one question to ask about any data-based decision.
2. Is data always neutral?
3. Write one rule for our class data charter.

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
