# Teacher guide — Grade 8, Unit 7: Telling the story

**Time:** 3 lessons + project (about 5 periods). **Track:** A (no-code).

| # | Lesson | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Claim, evidence, reasoning | 40–45 min |
| 2 | Poster design | 40–45 min |
| 3 | Feedback and presenting | 40–45 min |
| P | Unit project: Our class, our school poster | 2 periods |

## Before you start
* Materials: Poster paper, markers, sticky notes for feedback.
* Read `docs/pedagogy/lesson-anatomy.md` and `docs/pedagogy/ai-use-policy.md`.

## If you have no computers / no internet
All eight units run on paper, calculators and the blackboard. Computers are optional (spreadsheet or CODAP, https://codap.concord.org); every activity below has a paper version.

## Common misconceptions
* A poster full of graphs without a claim. Every graph needs a reason to be there.
* Claims that go beyond the data ("all students" from 10 classmates).

## Differentiation
* Support: give a partially filled table / sentence starters; pair with a data-steward role.
* Stretch: ask "what would change your conclusion?" and "who is missing?"

## Answer key (check questions)
**Lesson 1: Claim, evidence, reasoning**
1. What is evidence?  
   *Answer:* Data (numbers, graph) that support the claim.
2. What does reasoning add?  
   *Answer:* It explains how the evidence supports the claim and its limits.
3. 10 classmates walk. Claim: 'Nepali students walk to school.' OK?  
   *Answer:* No: sample is too small and not fair; claim too general.

**Lesson 2: Poster design**
1. What goes in the headline?  
   *Answer:* The main message/claim.
2. Name two things every graph needs.  
   *Answer:* Title and labelled axes (with units).
3. Why add a 'what we cannot say' box?  
   *Answer:* To be honest about limits and avoid overclaiming.

**Lesson 3: Feedback and presenting**
1. What is a 'wish' in feedback?  
   *Answer:* A specific suggestion for improvement.
2. What three things go in a 1-minute talk?  
   *Answer:* Claim, evidence, one limit.
3. Why avoid saying only 'good' or 'bad'?  
   *Answer:* It does not help the person improve.

## Alignment
Extends the Grade 8 compulsory Maths statistics chapter (pie chart, mean, median, mode) with question-forming, data collection and interpretation.

## Answers: practice exercises
1. (a) E (b) C (c) R.
2. e.g. "Most of us walk to school"; "Maths and Social are our favourite subjects"; "Half of us use a phone for more
   than two hours a day" (numbers must come from data).
3. Claim: walking is the most common way to reach school in our class. Evidence: 26 of 36 students (72%) walk; the
   next most common, bus, is 5 (14%) (class survey, synthetic table). Reasoning: walking is the mode by a wide
   margin; limitation: one class, one survey; travel mode may change in the monsoon.
4. (a) "17 of our 20 friends said they love momo" (or whatever the count was); (b) "In our class, students who use
   phones more tended to have lower marks; we cannot tell which causes which"; (c) "Our results were higher than two
   nearby schools last year."
5. Stars are hard to find: perhaps "you used real data", "you ended with a recommendation". Wishes: headline as a
   claim; one bar chart instead of three 8-colour pies; add source; cut the paragraph to two lines.
6. ~120 words: claim, the 26 of 36 (72%) figure pointing at the bar, a limitation, a next step.
7. (b): it is specific, says which way the bias pushes and why. (a) is true of all data and tells the reader nothing.
8. "Smarter" is not what literacy measures (literacy is reading/writing ability, linked to access to schools); and
   the table is synthetic. Better: "In this synthetic table, Hill districts have a higher median literacy rate
   (77.5%) than Mountain districts (65.5%)."
9. 17 of 36 (47%) sleep under 8 hours; median 8.05 hours. Not supported: fewer than half are under 8 hours. CER:
   "About half our class (17 of 36) sleeps less than 8 hours on school nights; the median is just above 8 hours."
   Good discussion: the same claim can be true for one class and false for another.
