# Grade 8 · Unit 7: Telling the story

> Track: A (no-code) · Tools: paper, poster paper · Lessons: 3 · Version: 0.1 draft (English)
> Datasets: `class-survey-synthetic`, `census-districts`

**Big idea.** Numbers matter when they support a claim people can check: claim, evidence, reasoning.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can write a claim supported by evidence and reasoning.
* I can design a clear one-page poster.
* I can give and receive feedback.

**Words to know:** claim, evidence, reasoning, poster, caption (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

---

## Lesson 1: Claim, evidence, reasoning

**Hook (5 min).** 'Our class sleeps too little.' Is that a claim? What proves or disproves it?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A **claim** answers the question. **Evidence** is the data. **Reasoning** links them and says why the evidence supports the claim — and how sure we are.

**Activity (20 min).** Take three claims; for each, find (or invent a plausible) evidence line and write the reasoning in one sentence. Mark claims that go further than the evidence.

**Check (5 min).**
1. What is evidence?
2. What does reasoning add?
3. 10 classmates walk. Claim: 'Nepali students walk to school.' OK?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Poster design

**Hook (5 min).** Walk past two posters. One you read in 10 seconds; one you cannot. What differs?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** One main message as a headline, one or two clean graphs, short captions, data source and date, and a 'what we cannot say' box.

**Activity (20 min).** Sketch a poster layout; trade sketches and give two stars and one wish.

**Check (5 min).**
1. What goes in the headline?
2. Name two things every graph needs.
3. Why add a 'what we cannot say' box?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Feedback and presenting

**Hook (5 min).** How do you feel when someone says 'wrong!'? What is a more helpful way?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Good feedback is specific, kind and useful: two stars (what works) and a wish (what to try next). Presenters explain the claim, evidence and one limit in under a minute.

**Activity (20 min).** Rehearse a 1-minute talk; give feedback to a partner using stars and a wish.

**Check (5 min).**
1. What is a 'wish' in feedback?
2. What three things go in a 1-minute talk?
3. Why avoid saying only 'good' or 'bad'?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
