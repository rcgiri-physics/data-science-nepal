# Grade 8 · Unit 1: What is data?

> Track: A (no-code) · Tools: paper, calculator · Lessons: 3 · Version: 0.1 draft (English)
> Datasets: `class-survey-synthetic`

**Big idea.** Data are recorded facts about people, places or things. Before we analyse anything we must know what one row is, what each column measures, and who collected it.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can tell numerical from categorical data.
* I can name who collects some of Nepal's official data.
* I can read a small table: rows, columns, units.

**Words to know:** data, variable, category, number, row, column, unit (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

---

## Lesson 1: Data are everywhere

**Hook (5 min).** Write five facts about yourself that someone could record on a form.

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A *variable* is something that can differ between people or things. Numerical variables are counted or measured (age, minutes to school). Categorical variables put things in groups (favourite subject, village).

**Activity (20 min).** In groups, sort 20 cards (height, mother tongue, ward number, shoe size, bus or walk, rainfall in mm…) into 'numerical' and 'categorical'. Argue about the tricky ones (ward number!).

**Check (5 min).**
1. Is 'favourite colour' numerical or categorical?
2. Give one numerical variable about your school.
3. Why is a ward number categorical even though it looks like a number?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Who collects Nepal's data?

**Hook (5 min).** Who tells us how many people live in Nepal? Who tells us tomorrow's weather? How do they know?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Official data come from organisations: the National Statistics Office (census, surveys), the Department of Hydrology and Meteorology (weather), the Ministry of Education and its centres (schools). Knowing the source helps us judge trust and purpose.

**Activity (20 min).** Match 8 questions (How many students are in Grade 8 in my district? How much rain fell in July?…) to the organisation that probably collects the answer. Then pick one and write what question the collector wanted to answer.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Which organisation runs the national census?
2. Why should we ask 'who collected this data and why'?
3. Name one thing a school could measure itself.

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Reading a table

**Hook (5 min).** Look at a table of five students. What is one row? What is one column?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** In a tidy table each **row** is one thing (one student) and each **column** is one variable. Units tell us how it was measured (cm, minutes). A table without units is hard to trust.

**Activity (20 min).** Use the printed 10-row class table (worksheet w2). Circle one row; underline one column; write the unit of each numerical column; say what question the table could answer.

**Check (5 min).**
1. A table has columns: student_code, travel_mode, travel_minutes, height_cm. How many variables?
2. Which of those are numerical?
3. What does one row represent?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
