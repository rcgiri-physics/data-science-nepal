# Grade 8 · Unit 4: Showing data

> Track: A (no-code); optional CODAP · Tools: graph paper, optional CODAP · Lessons: 3
> Datasets: `class-survey-synthetic`, `census-districts`

**Big idea.** A graph is an argument made visible. The right graph helps the eye; a careless one can mislead.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can draw a bar chart, dot plot and line graph from a table.
* I can choose a graph that fits the data.
* I can spot a misleading graph.

**Words to know:** bar chart, pie chart, dot plot, line graph, axis, scale, title (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

---

## Lesson 1: Bar charts and dot plots

**Hook (5 min).** Which is easier to compare: a list of 20 numbers or a picture of them?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Bar charts show counts or values for categories; dot plots show each observation of a numerical variable along a line, so you can see spread and clusters.

**Activity (20 min).** Draw a bar chart for travel mode and a dot plot of travel minutes from the class table (or in CODAP: drag attributes to the axes).

**Check (5 min).**
1. Which graph suits 'favourite subject'?
2. What can a dot plot show that a table hides?
3. What must every graph have?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Line graphs and pie charts

**Hook (5 min).** Temperature each day for a week, or the share of students who walk — which graph fits?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Line graphs show change over time. Pie charts show parts of a whole (the parts add to 100%). Don't use a pie for values that don't form a whole.

**Activity (20 min).** Plot a week of temperatures as a line graph; convert the travel-mode frequencies to percentages and draw a pie chart.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Which graph shows change over days?
2. Walk 12, bus 5, cycle 3 of 20: percent who walk?
3. Why would a pie chart of 'minutes by student' be wrong?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Misleading graphs

**Hook (5 min).** A newspaper bar chart shows Ward A looking five times taller than Ward B. The numbers are 52 and 50. How?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Graphs mislead when the axis does not start at zero, scales are uneven, 3D hides sizes, or the title claims more than the data show. Always read the axis first.

**Activity (20 min).** Examine 4 printed 'bad' graphs; list the trick in each and redraw one honestly.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Axis starts at 48 instead of 0 — effect?
2. Name one more way to mislead with a graph.
3. What is the first thing you check on a graph?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
