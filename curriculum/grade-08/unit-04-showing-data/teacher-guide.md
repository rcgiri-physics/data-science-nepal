# Teacher guide — Grade 8, Unit 4: Showing data

**Time:** 3 lessons + project (about 5 periods). **Track:** A (no-code); optional CODAP.

| # | Lesson | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bar charts and dot plots | 40–45 min |
| 2 | Line graphs and pie charts | 40–45 min |
| 3 | Misleading graphs | 40–45 min |
| P | Unit project: Graph gallery | 2 periods |

## Before you start
* Materials: Graph paper, rulers, coloured pencils; optional computers with CODAP.
* Read `docs/pedagogy/lesson-anatomy.md` and `docs/pedagogy/ai-use-policy.md`.

## If you have no computers / no internet
All eight units run on paper, calculators and the blackboard. Computers are optional (spreadsheet or CODAP, https://codap.concord.org); every activity below has a paper version.

## Common misconceptions
* Using a pie chart for numbers that are not parts of a whole.
* Starting a bar chart's axis above zero makes small differences look big.

## Differentiation
* Support: give a partially filled table / sentence starters; pair with a data-steward role.
* Stretch: ask "what would change your conclusion?" and "who is missing?"

## Answer key (check questions)
**Lesson 1: Bar charts and dot plots**
1. Which graph suits 'favourite subject'?  
   *Answer:* A bar chart (categories).
2. What can a dot plot show that a table hides?  
   *Answer:* Where values cluster, gaps and unusual values.
3. What must every graph have?  
   *Answer:* A title, labelled axes with units, and a sensible scale.

**Lesson 2: Line graphs and pie charts**
1. Which graph shows change over days?  
   *Answer:* A line graph.
2. Walk 12, bus 5, cycle 3 of 20: percent who walk?  
   *Answer:* 60%.
3. Why would a pie chart of 'minutes by student' be wrong?  
   *Answer:* The values are not parts of a single whole.

**Lesson 3: Misleading graphs**
1. Axis starts at 48 instead of 0 — effect?  
   *Answer:* Small differences look huge.
2. Name one more way to mislead with a graph.  
   *Answer:* Uneven scale, cut-off data, 3D, cherry-picked time period, misleading title.
3. What is the first thing you check on a graph?  
   *Answer:* The axes: what is measured and where they start.
