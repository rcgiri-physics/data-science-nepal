# Grade 8 · Unit 3: Collecting and recording

> Track: A (no-code) · Tools: paper, tally sheets, optional spreadsheet · Lessons: 3 · Version: 0.1 draft (English)
> Datasets: `class-survey-synthetic`

**Big idea.** Good data start with consent, careful recording and a clean table. Messy recording makes every later step harder.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can collect data with consent.
* I can use tally marks and frequency tables.
* I can record data in a tidy table with units.

**Words to know:** consent, tally, frequency, tidy table, anonymous (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

---

## Lesson 1: Consent and privacy

**Hook (5 min).** Would you be happy if your height and your family's income were pinned on the wall with your name?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** People have the right to say no. We tell them why we collect data, what we will do with it, and we avoid names: we use codes (S01, S02…).

**Activity (20 min).** Role-play asking for consent; write a 2-line consent statement; assign anonymous codes to all students.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Why do we use codes instead of names?
2. What should a consent statement say?
3. Is it ok to publish a table of each student's weight with names?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Tally and frequency

**Hook (5 min).** Count how many of you walk, take a bus or cycle. How do you keep track without losing count?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A *tally* records each observation with a mark (groups of five: ||||/). A *frequency table* lists each category and its count; the total must equal the number of observations.

**Activity (20 min).** Run the class travel-mode survey with tally sheets; build a frequency table; check the total.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Tally for 7 is…
2. In a frequency table the counts must add to…
3. Frequencies: walk 12, bus 5, cycle 3. How many students?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: A tidy table

**Hook (5 min).** Two students recorded 'travel: 20 min' and 'travel: half an hour'. Can we compare them easily?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A tidy table has one row per student, one column per variable, one unit per column, and no empty or mixed cells. Decide the unit first (minutes) and stick to it.

**Activity (20 min).** Convert the class's raw paper answers into a tidy table (code, travel_mode, travel_minutes, siblings); swap with a neighbour to check for mixed units or blanks.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Why should every value in travel_minutes use minutes?
2. What should one row be in our class table?
3. A blank cell means…

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
