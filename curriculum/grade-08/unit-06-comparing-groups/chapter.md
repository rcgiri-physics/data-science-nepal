# Grade 8 · Unit 6: Comparing groups

> Track: A (no-code) · Tools: paper, calculator · Lessons: 3 · Version: 0.1 draft (English)
> Datasets: `census-districts`

**Big idea.** Comparing two places fairly means using rates or percentages, not raw counts. District tables are for comparing.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can compute a rate or percentage to compare groups.
* I can compare two groups with a summary and a graph.
* I can avoid unfair comparisons.

**Words to know:** rate, percentage, per 1,000, compare, fair comparison (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

---

## Lesson 1: Counts versus rates

**Hook (5 min).** District P has 900 girls in school, district Q has 600. Which is doing better at girls' enrolment?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Counts depend on size. A *rate* divides by the size of the group: girls enrolled ÷ girls of school age. Use percent or per 1,000.

**Activity (20 min).** From the district table extract take 6 districts. Rank them by the count of people living abroad (absentee_population), then compute the rate absentee_population ÷ population × 100 and rank again; notice the two rankings differ.

**Check (5 min).**
1. District P: 900 of 1,800 children enrolled; Q: 600 of 1,000. Which has the higher enrolment rate?
2. Why is a count unfair across districts?
3. Convert 45 per 1,000 to a percent.

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Comparing two groups

**Hook (5 min).** Do Terai districts have higher literacy than Mountain districts in this table? How would you show it?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Compare groups with the same summary (median) and side-by-side graphs. Say how big the difference is and whether it is consistent.

**Activity (20 min).** Split the 10-district extract by ecological belt; compute the median literacy for each belt; draw two dot plots on one axis; write a comparison sentence.

**Check (5 min).**
1. What do you compute for each group before comparing?
2. If belt medians are 66 and 77, what is the difference?
3. Why draw both on the same axis?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Percent change and percentage points

**Hook (5 min).** Literacy rose from 60% to 66%. Is that '6% more' or '10% more'?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A change of 6 *percentage points* is a different thing from a 10% *relative* change (6 ÷ 60). Name the one you use.

**Activity (20 min).** Practise with three pairs of values (e.g. 60→66, 80→84, 50→45); say each as points and as percent change; discuss which sounds bigger in a headline.

**Check (5 min).**
1. 60% to 66%: change in percentage points?
2. Same change as relative percent?
3. Why do headlines sometimes pick the bigger-sounding one?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
