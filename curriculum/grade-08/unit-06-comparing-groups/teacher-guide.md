# Teacher guide — Grade 8, Unit 6: Comparing groups

**Time:** 3 lessons + project (about 5 periods). **Track:** A (no-code).

| # | Lesson | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Counts versus rates | 40–45 min |
| 2 | Comparing two groups | 40–45 min |
| 3 | Percent change and percentage points | 40–45 min |
| P | Unit project: Two places, fair comparison | 2 periods |

## Before you start
* Materials: Printed extract of the (synthetic) district table: 10 districts; calculators.
* Read `docs/pedagogy/lesson-anatomy.md` and `docs/pedagogy/ai-use-policy.md`.

## If you have no computers / no internet
All eight units run on paper, calculators and the blackboard. Computers are optional (spreadsheet or CODAP, https://codap.concord.org); every activity below has a paper version.

## Common misconceptions
* A bigger district with more school children is 'better at education'. Compare shares, not counts.
* Percent change vs percentage points.

## Differentiation
* Support: give a partially filled table / sentence starters; pair with a data-steward role.
* Stretch: ask "what would change your conclusion?" and "who is missing?"

## Answer key (check questions)
**Lesson 1: Counts versus rates**
1. District P: 900 of 1,800 children enrolled; Q: 600 of 1,000. Which has the higher enrolment rate?  
   *Answer:* P 50%, Q 60% → Q.
2. Why is a count unfair across districts?  
   *Answer:* Bigger districts have bigger counts just by being bigger.
3. Convert 45 per 1,000 to a percent.  
   *Answer:* 4.5%.

**Lesson 2: Comparing two groups**
1. What do you compute for each group before comparing?  
   *Answer:* The same summary, e.g. the median.
2. If belt medians are 66 and 77, what is the difference?  
   *Answer:* 11 percentage points.
3. Why draw both on the same axis?  
   *Answer:* To compare fairly at a glance.

**Lesson 3: Percent change and percentage points**
1. 60% to 66%: change in percentage points?  
   *Answer:* 6 points.
2. Same change as relative percent?  
   *Answer:* 6 ÷ 60 = 10%.
3. Why do headlines sometimes pick the bigger-sounding one?  
   *Answer:* To make a change seem more dramatic; always ask which one is used.
