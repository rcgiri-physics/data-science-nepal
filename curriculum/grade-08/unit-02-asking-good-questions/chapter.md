# Grade 8 · Unit 2: Asking good questions

> Track: A (no-code) · Tools: paper · Lessons: 3
> Datasets: `class-survey-synthetic`

**Big idea.** A good statistical question can be answered with data and expects variation in the answers. How we ask changes what we learn.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can tell a statistical question from a non-statistical one.
* I can spot a leading or biased question.
* I can choose between asking people and measuring.

**Words to know:** statistical question, survey, measurement, bias, sample, population (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

---

## Lesson 1: Statistical or not?

**Hook (5 min).** Which can data answer: 'How old is Ram?' or 'How old are the students in our school?'

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A statistical question expects different answers from different people or times. 'How long does it take Grade 8 students to reach school?' is statistical; 'How long does it take me today?' is not.

**Activity (20 min).** Sort 12 question strips into statistical / not statistical; rewrite three non-statistical ones to make them statistical.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Is 'What is the height of Mount Everest?' statistical?
2. Make 'How many siblings does Sita have?' statistical.
3. Why must a statistical question allow variation?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Surveys, measurements and bias

**Hook (5 min).** Ask: 'Don't you agree our canteen food is terrible?' What answer is the question pushing?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** To learn about a group we can ask (survey) or measure. Questions can be *leading* (push an answer) or *unclear*. Who we ask can also bias the result: asking only students in front-row seats misses the rest.

**Activity (20 min).** Rewrite three leading questions neutrally. Then measure 'time to write your name 5 times' for pairs, and discuss why survey answers and measurements may disagree.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Rewrite 'Isn't maths the best subject?' neutrally.
2. Name one way a sample can be biased.
3. Give one thing better measured than asked.

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Fair samples

**Hook (5 min).** We want to know how Grade 8 students travel to school. We only ask the 5 students beside us. What goes wrong?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A sample is a part of the population. A *random* sample (everyone has an equal chance, e.g. drawing roll numbers from a box) is fair. A convenience sample (whoever is nearby) may be biased.

**Activity (20 min).** Draw 6 roll numbers from a box to make a random sample of the class; compare their average travel time with the 6 nearest students. Record both and discuss.

**Check (5 min).**
1. What is a population?
2. Why is drawing names from a box fair?
3. Can a large sample still be biased?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
