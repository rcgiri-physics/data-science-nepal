## If you have no computers / no internet
All eight units run on paper, calculators and the blackboard. Computers are optional (spreadsheet or CODAP, https://codap.concord.org); every activity below has a paper version.

## Common misconceptions
* "How tall am I?" is a statistical question. It has one answer; 'How tall are Grade 8 students?' expects variation.
* More answers always means a better sample. A big biased sample is still biased.

## Differentiation
* Support: give a partially filled table / sentence starters; pair with a data-steward role.
* Stretch: ask "what would change your conclusion?" and "who is missing?"

## Answer key (check questions)
**Lesson 1: Statistical or not?**
1. Is 'What is the height of Mount Everest?' statistical?  
   *Answer:* No: one fixed answer.
2. Make 'How many siblings does Sita have?' statistical.  
   *Answer:* 'How many siblings do students in our class have?'
3. Why must a statistical question allow variation?  
   *Answer:* If every answer is the same there is nothing to analyse or compare.

**Lesson 2: Surveys, measurements and bias**
1. Rewrite 'Isn't maths the best subject?' neutrally.  
   *Answer:* 'Which subject do you like most?' (with options).
2. Name one way a sample can be biased.  
   *Answer:* e.g. asking only one section, only friends, only volunteers.
3. Give one thing better measured than asked.  
   *Answer:* e.g. height, travel time (use a clock).

**Lesson 3: Fair samples**
1. What is a population?  
   *Answer:* The whole group we want to learn about.
2. Why is drawing names from a box fair?  
   *Answer:* Everyone has an equal chance, reducing bias.
3. Can a large sample still be biased?  
   *Answer:* Yes, if the way we choose it favours some people.
