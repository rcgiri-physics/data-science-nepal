# Grade 9 · Unit 7: Percent, rate and per-capita

> Track: A/B · Tools: calculator, spreadsheet or pandas · Lessons: 3
> Datasets: `census-districts`

**Big idea.** Fair comparison needs rates. Per-capita and per-1,000 measures remove the effect of size; percentage points and percent change are not the same.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can compute shares, rates and per-capita values.
* I can distinguish percentage points from percent change.
* I can choose a denominator carefully.

**Words to know:** denominator, per capita, rate, share, percentage point (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

**Notebook:** `notebooks/rates.ipynb` (open in JupyterLite, Colab or Jupyter; runs offline on the synthetic table).

## Lesson 1: Share, percent and per 1,000

**Hook (5 min).** Which is more informative: '4,000 people abroad' or '4 per 1,000'?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A share = part ÷ whole. Multiply by 100 for a percent, by 1,000 for 'per 1,000'. Use rates to compare places of different sizes.

**Activity (PRIMM: Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make) (20 min).** Convert counts to per-1,000 rates for 8 districts; rank by count and by rate; discuss differences.

**Check (5 min).**
1. 12 of 200 as a percent?
2. 12 of 200 per 1,000?
3. Why per 1,000 for rare things?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Choosing a denominator

**Hook (5 min).** Girls enrolled ÷ what? All children? Girls of school age? Girls in the district?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** The denominator defines the question. 'Girls' share of enrolment' uses all enrolled; 'girls' enrolment rate' uses girls of school age (a different table).

**Activity (PRIMM: Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make) (20 min).** For three indicators, write the right denominator; compute two versions of one indicator and compare.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Girls' share of enrolment divides by…
2. Why can two 'girls' percentages differ?
3. Always state the denominator in…

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Percent change and percentage points

**Hook (5 min).** Enrolment share grew from 48% to 50%. Big or small?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Difference in percentages is in percentage points; relative change divides by the starting value. Report both when it matters.

**Activity (PRIMM: Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make) (20 min).** Compute both for five before/after pairs; write a fair headline for each.

**Check (5 min).**
1. 48% → 50%: points?
2. Relative change?
3. Fair headline wording?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
