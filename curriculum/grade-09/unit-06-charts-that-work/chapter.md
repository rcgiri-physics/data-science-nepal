# Grade 9 · Unit 6: Charts that work

> Track: B (code: matplotlib) · Tools: Python, matplotlib · Lessons: 3
> Datasets: `census-districts`

**Big idea.** Choose the chart that fits the question — histogram for distribution, bar for categories, scatter for relationships — and label it honestly.

**What you will be able to do**

* I can draw a histogram, bar chart and scatter plot.
* I can label axes, titles and units.
* I can read a box plot and spot outliers.

**Words to know:** histogram, bin, scatter plot, box plot, outlier (Nepali glossary: `curriculum/framework/glossary.md`).

## Lesson 1: Histograms and bar charts

**Hook (5 min).** What is the difference between a bar chart of belts and a histogram of literacy?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** Bar charts count categories. Histograms split a numerical range into bins and count values in each bin; the bin width changes the picture.

**Activity (PRIMM: Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make) (20 min).** Draw both for the district table; change bins from 5 to 20 and describe what changes.

**Check (5 min).**
1. Bars or histogram for 'belt'?
2. What is a bin?
3. Why try different bin counts?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 2: Box plots and outliers

**Hook (5 min).** How can one picture compare literacy across three belts?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A box shows the middle half of values (quartiles) with a line at the median; whiskers and dots show spread and outliers.

**Activity (PRIMM: Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make) (20 min).** Draw box plots by belt; identify one outlier; decide whether to investigate or keep it.

**Check (5 min).**
1. What does the line in the box show?
2. What is an outlier?
3. Should you delete outliers automatically?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?

## Lesson 3: Scatter plots

**Hook (5 min).** Do districts with more schools per person have higher literacy?

**Concept (≤ 5 min).** A scatter plot shows each case as a point to reveal a relationship. A pattern is not proof of cause; other factors may explain it.

**Activity (PRIMM: Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make) (20 min).** Plot two variables; describe direction (up/down), strength and any odd points; propose one other factor.

**Check (5 min).**
1. What does each dot represent?
2. If points slope upward, what do we call it?
3. Does it prove one causes the other?

**Reflect (2 min).** What surprised me? Who or what is missing from this data?
